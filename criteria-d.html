What this criterion asks

In Criterion D you collect evidence that shows whether your solution works and who it helps. You return to the success criteria you wrote in Criterion B1 and judge each one with data from your tests.

The four strands

StrandWhat you must showWhere it comes from
D1 Testing methodsDifferent testing methods to test your product, explained: why you feel each method is valid and useful, and what type of results you can expect from it.The success criteria in B1
D2 Success of the prototypeThe success of the prototype model evaluated against each of the success criteria (B1) by analysing the outcome of the testing methods described in D1.D1 results and your Criterion C build
D3 ImprovementsImprovements are listed and fully justified against the evaluation provided in D2.Criteria not fully met in D2
D4 Impact on the clientThe impact the product has on the client, detailed by referring to the client's problem from Criterion A.The client and problem in A1

These strands follow your class rubric. Check the full level descriptors in your summative document and with your teacher.

How D links to A, B and C

A gives the client and problem (used in D4). B1 gives the success criteria (each one is tested in D1 and judged in D2). C gives the prototype you test. D3 then feeds back into a better design. Every topic below has a complete worked sample that follows this chain.

How to approach it

  1. Copy your B1 success criteria. Next to each one, write the test you will run, how many trials and the pass mark (D1).
  2. Run the tests, record every result in a table and add photos or screenshots of the evidence (D1, D2).
  3. For each criterion say met, partly met or not met, with the numbers that show it (D2).
  4. Choose the three biggest weaknesses and say what you would change and why (D3).
  5. Ask your client for feedback. Say who benefits, and whether anything could go wrong (D4).
  6. Add your writing and pictures to each strand in the workspace below.
