Design Brief

Context that frames the whole Criterion B response for this lesson.

Problem context (real world & UAE link): After a flood or sandstorm in a UAE neighbourhood, debris such as kerbs, pipes and fallen palm fronds blocks the road. A rescue robot must detect the obstacle early and climb it without tipping.

Design task: Design a Python-controlled solution on the ready UGOT kit robot that uses the UGOT Time-of-Flight (TOF) sensor + gyro/IMU + drive motors. It must be programmable, testable and demonstrable by a Grade 10 student in this unit. This is a Digital Design unit: there is no 3D printing or laser cutting.

User: a 14–16 year-old MYP student building, coding, testing and demonstrating the robot in class and at the AIA STEM showcase.

B2 · Design Ideas 3 different ideas

Three ideas that differ in mechanism and form, not just looks. Each sketch is an isometric render of the 3D CAD model with the sensor and mount labelled.

Concept 1 — “Crawler-Tread”

Twin caterpillar tracks

Two long tread pods give a big contact patch that spreads weight over rubble. Simple and stable, but the tracks cannot lift over tall steps.

Envelope 228.0 × 210.0 × 66.0 mm · ≈ 283 g · ≈ AED 62

Concept 2 — “Spider-Stepper”

Six-legged walker

A hexapod body with servo hips steps over obstacles like an insect. Very futuristic, but six servos push cost and build time up.

Envelope 197.6 × 226.0 × 97.0 mm · ≈ 159 g · ≈ AED 47

Concept 3 — “Flipper-Lift” SELECTED

Front lifting flippers

Two powered flippers rotate down to lever the nose up over a step, while a gyro module keeps the body level. Fewest parts for the most climb.

Envelope 346.0 × 210.0 × 72.2 mm · ≈ 87 g · ≈ AED 31

3. Wireframe of the operator screen

#ElementWhat it does
1Distance (cm) gaugeThe main view. Shows live data from the robot so the operator can see what the program sees.
2Tilt (°) gaugeShows a number from the program so the operator can check it against the test pass mark.
3State label: DRIVE / CLIMB / STOPTells the operator what the program is doing now.
4STOP buttonStops the robot at once. Always visible and the biggest button.

4. Pseudocode

SET STOP_CM = 15, MAX_TILT = 30
DRIVE forward
WHILE distance > STOP_CM: keep driving
STOP, RAISE flipper, DRIVE slowly
IF tilt > MAX_TILT: STOP and LOWER flipper
PRINT distance, tilt, result

Pseudocode is plain English with the structure of the code. It must match the flowchart step for step.

Build and test the robot

No 3D printing or laser cutting. Use the ready UGOT kit, write the program in Python and test it against your B1 specification.

  1. Check the kit: UGOT transforming car, TOF module, flipper arm, laptop. Tell your teacher about any missing part now.
  2. Confirm your B3 design: the kit parts stand in for the “Flipper-Lift” sketch (front lifting flippers). The part you design is the program.
  3. Assemble with the kit's own instructions, then photograph the finished robot.
  4. Connect the laptop to the UGOT and run a short program: drive forward 2 seconds and stop.
  5. Function-test against the B1 function criteria: Climbs a step of at least 40 mm without tipping (gyro tilt stays below 35°). Record results in your portfolio.
  6. Carry on to Criterion C to plan, code, log and justify your changes.
