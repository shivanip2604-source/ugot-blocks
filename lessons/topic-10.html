Design Brief

Context that frames the whole Criterion B response for this lesson.

Problem context (real world & UAE link): At an airport gate or school entrance a robot recognises objects, faces or signs and shows a green tick or red cross to confirm what it sees.

Design task: Design a Python-controlled solution on the ready UGOT kit robot that uses the UGOT Camera with AI image recognition + display/servo. It must be programmable, testable and demonstrable by a Grade 10 student in this unit. This is a Digital Design unit: there is no 3D printing or laser cutting.

User: a 14–16 year-old MYP student building, coding, testing and demonstrating the robot in class and at the AIA STEM showcase.

B2 · Design Ideas 3 different ideas

Three ideas that differ in mechanism and form, not just looks. Each sketch is an isometric render of the 3D CAD model with the sensor and mount labelled.

Concept 1 — “Screen-Panel”

Tilting display with camera bezel

A tilting display panel with the camera in its top bezel, plus green and red LEDs to confirm or reject. Familiar and friendly.

Envelope 100.0 × 117.0 × 151.0 mm · ≈ 59 g · ≈ AED 27

Concept 2 — “Scanner-Arch” SELECTED

Overhead scanning arch

An arch holds a camera over a work tray so each object is scanned from the same angle and light. Most repeatable and best for training data.

Envelope 130.0 × 150.0 × 117.0 mm · ≈ 89 g · ≈ AED 37

Concept 3 — “Lens-Orb”

Spherical lens with holo emitter

A spherical housing with a big lens and a holo-cone emitter looks futuristic, but is harder to print and the housing is bulky.

Envelope 144.0 × 100.0 × 152.0 mm · ≈ 182 g · ≈ AED 42

4. Pseudocode

SET MIN_CONFIDENCE = 0.8
SWITCH TO object recognition
LOOP
  name, conf = best match
  IF name = target AND conf ≥ MIN: TICK, green
  ELSE CROSS, red
  WAIT 0.3 s
END LOOP

Pseudocode is plain English with the structure of the code. It must match the flowchart step for step.

Build and test the robot

No 3D printing or laser cutting. Use the ready UGOT kit, write the program in Python and test it against your B1 specification.

  1. Check the kit: UGOT camera, main controller display/light, servo, laptop. Tell your teacher about any missing part now.
  2. Confirm your B3 design: the kit parts stand in for the “Scanner-Arch” sketch (overhead scanning arch). The part you design is the program.
  3. Assemble with the kit's own instructions, then photograph the finished robot.
  4. Connect the laptop to the UGOT and run a short program: switch to AI vision and print what is recognised.
  5. Function-test against the B1 function criteria: Recognises a trained object in under 1 second at 200–500 mm (design target). Record results in your portfolio.
  6. Carry on to Criterion C to plan, code, log and justify your changes.
