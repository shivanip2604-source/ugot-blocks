A1 · Design need

Explain the problem in a real setting, with evidence, and justify why it needs a solution.

Large sites such as warehouses, school campuses and solar farms must be checked at night. Guards walking the same route every night face problems, including:

  1. The same route is boring and tiring: Walking the same route for hours makes guards less alert as the night goes on.
  2. Hazards can be missed: A blocked path, an open door or a leak is easy to miss at 3 a.m. in poor light.
  3. Large areas take a long time: Only a part of a big site can be checked in one round.
  4. There is no record of what was seen: Guards remember what they saw, but there is nothing to check afterwards.
  5. Night work puts guards at risk: Working alone in the dark exposes guards to falls and to people they may meet.

Patrols of warehouses, campuses and solar farms are repetitive and are often carried out at night (add a real source and the date you accessed it). A patrol that follows the same route accurately and reports what it sees would free guards for more important tasks.

Why a solution is needed: A reliable, repeatable night patrol would find problems sooner and keep guards out of danger.

A3 · Existing products

Study a range of similar products. For each: what it is, what works, what does not, and what you will take from it.

1. Security patrol robot (such as Knightscope K5)

A large outdoor robot that patrols car parks and campuses with cameras and sensors.

Works well
Patrols on its own and reports to a control centre.
Limits
Expensive and heavy; needs service.
What I will take from it
Patrol routes with reports.

2. Robot vacuum cleaner (such as Roomba)

A small home robot that maps a room and cleans it without help.

Works well
Cheap, repeatable route and obstacle sensors.
Limits
Made for floors indoors, not outdoor sites.
What I will take from it
Obstacle sensing and returning to base.

3. Guard-tour checkpoint system

Guards touch tags at fixed points around a site so their rounds are logged.

Works well
Simple and cheap; creates a record.
Limits
Relies on a person and only checks fixed points.
What I will take from it
A record of what was checked and when.

4. Patrol drone

A drone that flies a planned route over a large site with a camera.

Works well
Covers large areas quickly.
Limits
Short battery life; weather and flying rules.
What I will take from it
A camera view from above.

What this analysis tells me

These products come from very different fields, and none is designed for this client's exact setting. Comparing them shows many different ways of dealing with a similar problem. From them I will take:

  • Security patrol robot: Patrol routes with reports.
  • Robot vacuum cleaner: Obstacle sensing and returning to base.
  • Guard-tour checkpoint system: A record of what was checked and when.
  • Patrol drone: A camera view from above.

These ideas become the starting point for the three design concepts I sketch in B2.

A4 · Design brief

Summarise the problem, the client and your research. Cite the research codes and the B1 numbers in brackets, like (Ab and 7.1).

Facility managers of warehouses, campuses and solar farms that need night patrols, in a warehouse, school campus or solar farm in the UAE, face a real problem. Guards who walk the same route every night get tired and can miss hazards. The sites are large, there is no record of what was checked, and working alone in the dark carries risk. (taken from my A1 problem analysis). A reliable, repeatable night patrol would find problems sooner and keep guards out of danger.

My design brief is to develop a solution that helps facility managers of warehouses, campuses and solar farms that need night patrols deal with this problem. From my research I found that A realistic campus patrol loop was about 10 m in the lab test and 400 m on site (EXEMPLAR DATA) (Aa and 5.1, 7.1). I also found that about 3 in 10 trial runs clipped an obstacle placed within 300 mm of the route (EXEMPLAR DATA) (Ad and 7.2, 6.1). After studying a wide range of existing products I learned that security robots and robot vacuums follow mapped routes and avoid obstacles using sensors (secondary research) (Ab and 8.1, 7.2). My research shows that the solution needs to do the following. It must complete a 10 m patrol loop and avoid obstacles placed within 300 mm. (Ac and 7.1) It must see 360° around it, or two overlapping 180° views. (Ad and 7.2) It must stop if an obstacle is closer than 100 mm. (Af and 6.1) It also has to be affordable and practical. It must cost no more than AED 70 in added parts. (Ae and 2.1) It must be possible to build and test in one 70-minute lesson. (Ae and 3.2) In my survey most responses preferred this colour scheme: slate grey (Ag and 1.1), so I will use it.

The point of the design is to help facility managers of warehouses, campuses and solar farms that need night patrols and keep people safer. It is connected with the global context “Globalization and sustainability” because it responds to a real need in the community. It shows the Key Concept “Systems” because the parts of a solution have to work together as one system, and the Related Concepts “Function” and “Adaptation” because the solution will be judged by how well it works for the people who use it. (Check these against your own unit planner.)

From brief to specification. The brief points to the numbers in B1 (for example 7.1 and 7.2), where each need becomes a measurable success criterion. Criterion A does not choose a solution: that happens in B2 and B3.
