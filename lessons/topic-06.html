Design Brief

Context that frames the whole Criterion B response for this lesson.

Problem context (real world & UAE link): A robot guarding a school entrance or mosque car park needs to look around, zoom on a face or number plate and keep the picture steady.

Design task: Design a Python-controlled solution on the ready UGOT kit robot that uses the UGOT Camera module + pan/tilt servos. It must be programmable, testable and demonstrable by a Grade 10 student in this unit. This is a Digital Design unit: there is no 3D printing or laser cutting.

User: a 14–16 year-old MYP student building, coding, testing and demonstrating the robot in class and at the AIA STEM showcase.

2.2 Re-uses UGOT kit servos, sensors and fasteners; no proprietary parts.Avoids extra purchase orders and keeps the design compatible with kits the school already owns.
Customer3.1 Mounts on the UGOT robot using only the kit's own fixings and hand tools (check hole spacing against your own kit).Every group's UGOT kit must be able to accept the part.

Environment4.1 At least 80% of any added material is reused card, paper or kit parts; nothing new is printed or cut.Reused card and the kit's own parts follow the 6 R's (Reduce, Reuse) and need no fabrication machines.
4.2 Added mass on the UGOT robot ≤ 200 g.Lower mass puts less load on the UGOT motors and battery.
Size5.1 Overall envelope ≤ 120 × 130 × 150 mm (L × W × H).Keeps the module compact enough to mount on the UGOT chassis without hitting its wheels or the floor. From research: Aa
5.2 The robot and its test course fit on one standard classroom table (about 1.2 m × 0.8 m).The unit is tested at a normal desk in a single lesson, with no workshop machines.
Safety6.1 No exposed wiring; servo gaps are covered so fingers cannot be caught.Reduces the chance of injury to the student building, testing and demonstrating it. From research: Ad, Af
6.2 No exposed sharp corners: all edges filleted or sanded; sensor lens recessed about 2 mm to avoid impact cracks.Standard safe-handling requirement for a student-built robotic part. From research: Af
Function7.1 Pan covers 180° and tilt covers 60° in under 3 seconds.Directly measures whether the design solves the problem in the scenario above. From research: Aa, Ac
7.2 Camera view is never blocked by the housing across its full pan and tilt range.Sensor range figures are design targets; the housing must never block or shorten them. From research: Ab, Ad
7.3 The sensor's field of view is unobstructed by any part of the housing.A sensor that is blocked gives wrong data, so the design fails its main job.
7.4 Sensors and servos are replaceable with hand tools in under 2 minutes.Classroom robots are reused across cohorts, so parts will wear and break. From research: Ac
Material8.1 Uses only parts from the UGOT kit, plus recycled card for test props.Kit parts are reliable and already paid for; recycled card is free and quick to change. From research: Ab
8.2 Flat test props (cards, signs, tags) are printed on paper or card, not 3D-printed or laser-cut.Paper props take minutes, can be changed after every test and need no workshop machines.

B2 · Design Ideas 3 different ideas

Three ideas that differ in mechanism and form, not just looks. Each sketch is an isometric render of the 3D CAD model with the sensor and mount labelled.

Concept 1 — “Gimbal-Eye” SELECTED

Pan-tilt eyeball

A spherical eye in a yoke is moved by pan and tilt servos like a real eye, so the camera can look anywhere without the body moving.

Envelope 110.0 × 120.0 × 132.0 mm · ≈ 108 g · ≈ AED 38

Concept 2 — “Compound-Eye”

Insect dome of lens tubes

A dome of lens tubes copies an insect eye for a wide view. Eye-catching, but only one lens is a real camera so the rest are decoration.

Envelope 138.8 × 125.8 × 117.4 mm · ≈ 194 g · ≈ AED 43

Concept 3 — “Periscope-Cam”

Raised sliding mast camera

A camera on a motorised mast can rise above obstacles like a submarine periscope. Great view height, but a tall mast can tip the robot.

Envelope 100.0 × 80.0 × 193.0 mm · ≈ 74 g · ≈ AED 27

4. Pseudocode

SWITCH TO face recognition
REPEAT
  FOR each pan angle
    MOVE servo, WAIT 0.6 s
    IF face recognised: LIGHT green, TONE, STOP
UNTIL found

Build and test the robot

No 3D printing or laser cutting. Use the ready UGOT kit, write the program in Python and test it against your B1 specification.

  1. Check the kit: UGOT camera module, pan/tilt arm, main controller, laptop. Tell your teacher about any missing part now.
  2. Confirm your B3 design: the kit parts stand in for the “Gimbal-Eye” sketch (pan-tilt eyeball). The part you design is the program.
  3. Assemble with the kit's own instructions, then photograph the finished robot.
  4. Connect the laptop to the UGOT and run a short program: turn the camera to look left, centre and right.
  5. Function-test against the B1 function criteria: Pan covers 180° and tilt covers 60° in under 3 seconds. Record results in your portfolio.
  6. Carry on to Criterion C to plan, code, log and justify your changes.
