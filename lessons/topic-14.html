Design Brief

Context that frames the whole Criterion B response for this lesson.

Problem context (real world & UAE link): A robot delivers medicine to a hospital ward or a parcel to a door. It must keep the load level and drop it in exactly the right place.

Design task: Design a Python-controlled solution on the ready UGOT kit robot that uses the UGOT Gyro/IMU + height encoder + lift motor + servo. It must be programmable, testable and demonstrable by a Grade 10 student in this unit. This is a Digital Design unit: there is no 3D printing or laser cutting.

User: a 14–16 year-old MYP student building, coding, testing and demonstrating the robot in class and at the AIA STEM showcase.

4.2 Added mass on the UGOT robot ≤ 150 g.Lower mass puts less load on the UGOT motors and battery.
Size5.1 Overall envelope ≤ 160 × 120 × 80 mm (L × W × H).Keeps the module compact enough to mount on the UGOT chassis without hitting its wheels or the floor. From research: Aa
5.2 The robot and its test course fit on one standard classroom table (about 1.2 m × 0.8 m).The unit is tested at a normal desk in a single lesson, with no workshop machines.
Safety6.1 Lift arms have a guard so fingers cannot enter the scissor pinch points.Reduces the chance of injury to the student building, testing and demonstrating it. From research: Ad, Af
6.2 No exposed sharp corners: all edges filleted or sanded; sensor lens recessed about 2 mm to avoid impact cracks.Standard safe-handling requirement for a student-built robotic part. From research: Af
Function7.1 Lifts a 200 g parcel by 80 mm and stops within ±3 mm of the target height.Directly measures whether the design solves the problem in the scenario above. From research: Aa, Ac

Material8.1 Uses only parts from the UGOT kit, plus recycled card for test props.Kit parts are reliable and already paid for; recycled card is free and quick to change. From research: Ab
8.2 Flat test props (cards, signs, tags) are printed on paper or card, not 3D-printed or laser-cut.Paper props take minutes, can be changed after every test and need no workshop machines.

B2 · Design Ideas 3 different ideas

Three ideas that differ in mechanism and form, not just looks. Each sketch is an isometric render of the 3D CAD model with the sensor and mount labelled.

Concept 1 — “Scissor-Lift”

Scissor platform with height encoder

A scissor mechanism lifts a flat platform straight up and stays stable, while an encoder reports the exact height.

Envelope 138.0 × 107.0 × 158.0 mm · ≈ 130 g · ≈ AED 47

Concept 2 — “Gimbal-Cradle”

Self-levelling ring cradle

Two rings and a gyro keep the package level however the robot tilts. Beautiful for fragile loads, but heavier and lifts nothing.

Envelope 172.0 × 110.0 × 142.0 mm · ≈ 64 g · ≈ AED 29

Concept 3 — “Drop-Hatch” SELECTED

Hopper with bottom hatch

A hopper drops the cargo through a hinged hatch when a sensor confirms the drop zone. Simple, but the hatch cannot place gently.

Envelope 150.0 × 108.0 × 65.0 mm · ≈ 75 g · ≈ AED 30

Build and test the robot

No 3D printing or laser cutting. Use the ready UGOT kit, write the program in Python and test it against your B1 specification.

  1. Check the kit: UGOT mecanum car, arm, camera, TOF module, AprilTags, laptop. Tell your teacher about any missing part now.
  2. Confirm your B3 design: the kit parts stand in for the “Drop-Hatch” sketch (hopper with bottom hatch). The part you design is the program.
  3. Assemble with the kit's own instructions, then photograph the finished robot.
  4. Connect the laptop to the UGOT and run a short program: record two arm actions and replay them.
  5. Function-test against the B1 function criteria: Lifts a 200 g parcel by 80 mm and stops within ±3 mm of the target height. Record results in your portfolio.
  6. Carry on to Criterion C to plan, code, log and justify your changes.
