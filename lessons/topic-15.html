Design Brief

Context that frames the whole Criterion B response for this lesson.

Problem context (real world & UAE link): After a major incident the first responders need a small robot to locate casualties, show a live picture and carry medical supplies to them.

Design task: Design a Python-controlled solution on the ready UGOT kit robot that uses the UGOT Rescue camera + vitals/proximity sensor + servo + hoist. It must be programmable, testable and demonstrable by a Grade 10 student in this unit. This is a Digital Design unit: there is no 3D printing or laser cutting.

User: a 14–16 year-old MYP student building, coding, testing and demonstrating the robot in class and at the AIA STEM showcase.

Each success criterion is described and justified using ACCESSFM. Rows marked      are measurable. Size and mass are digital design figures from the CAD model: nothing is printed or cut.

Specification#Success criterionJustification
Aesthetics1.1 Colour scheme of rescue white with hazard-orange stripes and a red cross.A high-visibility, consistent palette makes the tool instantly recognisable and looks professional at the AIA STEM showcase. From research: Ag
1.2 Smooth, rounded external edges (fillet ≥ 2 mm) with no visible fasteners on the outer shell.Rounded edges read as safe to handle and avoid snagging. From research: Ag
Cost2.1 Estimated cost of added parts and test props per robot ≤ AED 70 (kit parts are already owned).The school already owns the UGOT kit, so only small extras such as card, tape and printed test cards count; keeps a class set affordable. From research: Ae
2.2 Re-uses UGOT kit servos, sensors and fasteners; no proprietary parts.Avoids extra purchase orders and keeps the design compatible with kits the school already owns.
Customer3.1 Mounts on the UGOT robot using only the kit's own fixings and hand tools (check hole spacing against your own kit).Every group's UGOT kit must be able to accept the part.
3.2 Assembles in one lesson (≤ 70 min) by a pair of Grade 10 students.Matches the AIA single-period lesson structure for this unit. From research: Ae
Environment4.1 At least 80% of any added material is reused card, paper or kit parts; nothing new is printed or cut.Reused card and the kit's own parts follow the 6 R's (Reduce, Reuse) and need no fabrication machines.
4.2 Added mass on the UGOT robot ≤ 100 g.Lower mass puts less load on the UGOT motors and battery.
Size5.1 Overall envelope ≤ 150 × 150 × 140 mm (L × W × H).Keeps the module compact enough to mount on the UGOT chassis without hitting its wheels or the floor. From research: Aa
5.2 The robot and its test course fit on one standard classroom table (about 1.2 m × 0.8 m).The unit is tested at a normal desk in a single lesson, with no workshop machines.
Safety6.1 Strobe lights and a siren warn bystanders; all edges are rounded and wings fold away.Reduces the chance of injury to the student building, testing and demonstrating it. From research: Ad, Af
6.2 No exposed sharp corners: all edges filleted or sanded; sensor lens recessed about 2 mm to avoid impact cracks.Standard safe-handling requirement for a student-built robotic part. From research: Af
Function7.1 Carries a 200 g medical kit for 5 m and delivers it to a person within 300 mm.Directly measures whether the design solves the problem in the scenario above. From research: Aa, Ac
7.2 Rescue camera streams a clear picture from up to 2 m away (design target).Sensor range figures are design targets; the housing must never block or shorten them. From research: Ab, Ad
7.3 The sensor's field of view is unobstructed by any part of the housing.A sensor that is blocked gives wrong data, so the design fails its main job.
7.4 Sensors and servos are replaceable with hand tools in under 2 minutes.Classroom robots are reused across cohorts, so parts will wear and break. From research: Ac
Material8.1 Uses only parts from the UGOT kit, plus recycled card for test props.Kit parts are reliable and already paid for; recycled card is free and quick to change. From research: Ab
8.2 Flat test props (cards, signs, tags) are printed on paper or card, not 3D-printed or laser-cut.Paper props take minutes, can be changed after every test and need no workshop machines.

B2 · Design Ideas 3 different ideas

Three ideas that differ in mechanism and form, not just looks. Each sketch is an isometric render of the 3D CAD model with the sensor and mount labelled.

Concept 1 — “Med-Capsule”

Sealed medical capsule

A capsule on skid rails carries the medical kit, with a beacon and vitals sensor. Clean and protective, but cannot reach over obstacles.

Concept 2 — “Crane-Boom”

Winch crane with hook

A rotating boom lifts and lowers a kit with a hook block, with the camera at the tip. Strong reach, but top-heavy and bulky.

Envelope 247.0 × 100.0 × 145.0 mm · ≈ 102 g · ≈ AED 33

Concept 3 — “Shield-Wing” SELECTED

Folding hazard shield wings

A central mast carries a camera and siren, with two servo-driven shield wings that fold out to protect the kit and warn people.

Envelope 130.2 × 138.1 × 122.0 mm · ≈ 62 g · ≈ AED 28

3. Wireframe of the operator screen

#ElementWhat it does
1Camera view with person boxThe main view. Shows live data from the robot so the operator can see what the program sees.
2State label SEARCH / APPROACH / DELIVER / DONETells the operator what the program is doing now.
3Distance (cm)Shows a number from the program so the operator can check it against the test pass mark.
4Emergency STOP buttonStops the robot at once. Always visible and the biggest button.

4. Pseudocode

SET state = SEARCH
WHILE state is not DONE
  IF SEARCH: IF person visible → APPROACH ELSE turn
  IF APPROACH: IF distance ≤ 30 → DELIVER ELSE drive
  IF DELIVER: LOWER, RELEASE, DONE
STOP

Pseudocode is plain English with the structure of the code. It must match the flowchart step for step.

Build and test the robot

No 3D printing or laser cutting. Use the ready UGOT kit, write the program in Python and test it against your B1 specification.

  1. Check the kit: UGOT car, camera, TOF module, arm or hoist, medical kit dummy, laptop. Tell your teacher about any missing part now.
  2. Confirm your B3 design: the kit parts stand in for the “Shield-Wing” sketch (folding hazard shield wings). The part you design is the program.
  3. Assemble with the kit's own instructions, then photograph the finished robot.
  4. Connect the laptop to the UGOT and run a short program: print the camera result and the distance.
  5. Function-test against the B1 function criteria: Carries a 200 g medical kit for 5 m and delivers it to a person within 300 mm. Record results in your portfolio.
  6. Carry on to Criterion C to plan, code, log and justify your changes.
