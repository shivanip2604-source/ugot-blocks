D1 · Testing methods

Describe the different testing methods you will use on the product. For each one, explain why it is valid and useful, and what kind of results you expect.

TestMethod and criteria it testsWhat I will do · trialsWhy this test is valid and usefulResults I expect
T1Visual checklist and client rating
Criteria: 1.1, 1.2		Compare the built model with the drawing using a tick list; ask the client to rate the look from 1–5.
1 build, 3 raters		Colour and shape are judged by looking, so a checklist makes the judgement fair and repeatable. A rating shows what the client thinks.Yes/no ticks and a score out of 5.
T2Cost and parts audit
Criteria: 2.1, 2.2, 8.1, 8.2		Keep a receipt tally of everything added, and tick every part against the kit inventory.
1 audit		Cost and parts are facts that can be counted. The tally shows exactly where the money goes.AED total and a parts list.
T3Build, fit and swap timing
Criteria: 3.1, 3.2, 5.2, 7.4		A pair builds the model with a stopwatch running; mount it; place it on the table; swap a sensor.
1 build, 1 swap		These are the real jobs a student pair must do in one lesson, so timing them shows if the design is practical.Minutes and seconds, pass/fail.
T4Mass and size measurement
Criteria: 4.1, 4.2, 5.1		Weigh the added parts on a kitchen scale; measure length, width and height with a ruler; work out the percentage reused.
3 measurements each		Scales and rulers give numbers that can be compared directly with the limits.Grams, millimetres, per cent.
T5Main function test
Criteria: 7.1		Lift 5 test objects (20, 50, 80, 120, 150 g) at 300 mm reach, 2 trials each. Record lift and drop.
10 lifts		The weights and reach come straight from the criterion and the result is pass or fail.All 5 objects lifted; drop rate under 1 in 10.
T6Second function test
Criteria: 7.2		Aim the TOF at a card at 40–2000 mm with the open claw in front; print three readings per distance.
5 distances × 3		Checks that the claw does not block the sensor. The test uses the exact numbers written in criterion 7.2, so the result answers it directly.Readings within 5% at every distance.
T7Safety and view check
Criteria: 6.1, 6.2, 7.3		Check pinch points and edges with a finger gauge and a fingertip; look along the sensor line for blocking parts.
Every edge		Safety must be checked on the real build, because drawings do not show every sharp edge.Pass/fail with a photo.
T8Client feedback interview
Criteria: All, and D4		Show the working model to the client and ask four set questions; record the answers.
1 client		The client decides if the product solves the problem in A1, so their view is needed for D2 and D4.Quotes and a 1–5 score.

Valid means the test really measures what the criterion asks. Useful means the result helps you decide what to improve. Eight tests together cover all 18 success criteria.

Level 7–8 check. ☐ More than one kind of test (measuring, timing, checklist, feedback). ☐ Each test says why it is valid and useful. ☐ Each test says what result to expect. ☐ Every B1 criterion has at least one test.

D3 · Improvements

List improvements and justify each one against the evaluation in D2. Say how you would check that it worked.

ImprovementProblem found in D2What I will changeJustificationHow I will re-test it
1. Add rubber fingertip pads7.1: both drops were heavy, round objectsGlue silicone or rubber pads (recycled) to each finger.Friction is the main reason round objects slip; pads add very little mass.Lift the 150 g block 10 times; aim for 10 of 10.
2. Add a gap stop at 6 mm6.1: gap closed to 5 mmAdd a small card stop so the claw cannot close below 6 mm unless gripping an object.Meets the safety criterion without losing grip on small items.Measure the closed gap 3 times.
3. Slow the last 20° of closing7.1: objects shifted as the claw snapped shutReduce servo speed near the grip point in Python.A gentler close reduces object movement and pinching force.Repeat lifts and compare drop rate.

D4 · Impact on the client

Explain the effect of the product on the client. Refer back to the client and problem from Criterion A.

The client and the problem (from A1)

Client: Rescue teams who must move light rubble, and the people trapped near it.
Problem: After a collapse or storm, light debris has to be moved before a casualty can be reached. Doing this by hand is dangerous, and existing tools struggle with irregular shapes and limited reach.

Client feedback EXEMPLAR

“It lifted the cardboard and the bottle without me going near it. The heavy round block was the problem.” (Rescue team member) EXEMPLAR

How the product helps solve that problem

  • Rescuers keep their hands out of unstable debris.
  • Light objects can be cleared quickly to reach a casualty.

Link to the need in A1: Moving debris safely reduces the risk to rescuers and shortens the time before a casualty is reached.

Wider community

  • Safer rescue work could reduce injuries to responders.

Things that could go wrong

  • A dropped object could hit the casualty.
  • Should only be used on light debris.
Level 7–8 check. ☐ The client and problem from A1 are named again. ☐ Feedback from the client is quoted. ☐ Benefits and risks are both explained. ☐ The wider community is considered.
