Design Brief

Context that frames the whole Criterion B response for this lesson.

Problem context (real world & UAE link): A delivery robot in a hospital or warehouse follows a painted line and reads wall tags to know which room it is in.

Design task: Design a Python-controlled solution on the ready UGOT kit robot that uses the UGOT Line-tracking sensors + tag-recognition camera. It must be programmable, testable and demonstrable by a Grade 10 student in this unit. This is a Digital Design unit: there is no 3D printing or laser cutting.

User: a 14–16 year-old MYP student building, coding, testing and demonstrating the robot in class and at the AIA STEM showcase.

Size5.1 Overall envelope ≤ 200 × 220 × 40 mm (L × W × H).Keeps the module compact enough to mount on the UGOT chassis without hitting its wheels or the floor. From research: Aa
5.2 The robot and its test course fit on one standard classroom table (about 1.2 m × 0.8 m).The unit is tested at a normal desk in a single lesson, with no workshop machines.
Safety6.1 Sensors sit flush so a bump cannot snap them off, and there are no sharp wing tips.Reduces the chance of injury to the student building, testing and demonstrating it. From research: Ad, Af
6.2 No exposed sharp corners: all edges filleted or sanded; sensor lens recessed about 2 mm to avoid impact cracks.Standard safe-handling requirement for a student-built robotic part. From research: Af
Function7.1 Follows a 20 mm wide line at 0.15 m/s with fewer than 2 line losses per lap.Directly measures whether the design solves the problem in the scenario above. From research: Aa, Ac
7.2 Camera reads a tag at 100–300 mm distance (design target; verify with your tags).Sensor range figures are design targets; the housing must never block or shorten them. From research: Ab, Ad
7.3 The sensor's field of view is unobstructed by any part of the housing.A sensor that is blocked gives wrong data, so the design fails its main job.
7.4 Sensors and servos are replaceable with hand tools in under 2 minutes.Classroom robots are reused across cohorts, so parts will wear and break. From research: Ac
Material8.1 Uses only parts from the UGOT kit, plus recycled card for test props.Kit parts are reliable and already paid for; recycled card is free and quick to change. From research: Ab
8.2 Flat test props (cards, signs, tags) are printed on paper or card, not 3D-printed or laser-cut.Paper props take minutes, can be changed after every test and need no workshop machines.

B2 · Design Ideas 3 different ideas

Three ideas that differ in mechanism and form, not just looks. Each sketch is an isometric render of the 3D CAD model with the sensor and mount labelled.

Concept 1 — “Line-Wing” SELECTED

Seven-sensor swept wing

A swept wing carries seven line sensors across the front so the robot sees the line early and corrects smoothly on curves.

Envelope 184.1 × 204.0 × 29.0 mm · ≈ 54 g · ≈ AED 26

Concept 2 — “Tag-Frame”

Tag camera gate

A tall frame holds a camera that reads tag panels at head height. Great for tags, but it does not follow lines.

Envelope 134.0 × 101.9 × 141.0 mm · ≈ 47 g · ≈ AED 25

Concept 3 — “Trace-Pointer”

Arrow body with line sensor and camera

A pointed chevron body with one line sensor underneath and a small tag camera on top, plus a marker holder for tracing routes. Compact, but one sensor loses curves.

Envelope 212.0 × 106.6 × 34.0 mm · ≈ 46 g · ≈ AED 25

Build and test the robot

No 3D printing or laser cutting. Use the ready UGOT kit, write the program in Python and test it against your B1 specification.

  1. Check the kit: UGOT mecanum car, camera, printed plates, laptop. Tell your teacher about any missing part now.
  2. Confirm your B3 design: the kit parts stand in for the “Line-Wing” sketch (seven-sensor swept wing). The part you design is the program.
  3. Assemble with the kit's own instructions, then photograph the finished robot.
  4. Connect the laptop to the UGOT and run a short program: print the licence plate text the camera sees.
  5. Function-test against the B1 function criteria: Follows a 20 mm wide line at 0.15 m/s with fewer than 2 line losses per lap. Record results in your portfolio.
  6. Carry on to Criterion C to plan, code, log and justify your changes.
