Design Brief

Context that frames the whole Criterion B response for this lesson.

Problem context (real world & UAE link): In a collapsed building or a crowded stadium car park, a trapped person can only call for help. A robot with directional microphones can turn towards the loudest sound and guide rescuers.

Design task: Design a Python-controlled solution on the ready UGOT kit robot that uses the UGOT Microphone / sound sensors + servo to aim. It must be programmable, testable and demonstrable by a Grade 10 student in this unit. This is a Digital Design unit: there is no 3D printing or laser cutting.

User: a 14–16 year-old MYP student building, coding, testing and demonstrating the robot in class and at the AIA STEM showcase.

Environment4.1 At least 80% of any added material is reused card, paper or kit parts; nothing new is printed or cut.Reused card and the kit's own parts follow the 6 R's (Reduce, Reuse) and need no fabrication machines.
4.2 Added mass on the UGOT robot ≤ 100 g.Lower mass puts less load on the UGOT motors and battery.
Size5.1 Overall envelope ≤ 180 × 160 × 130 mm (L × W × H).Keeps the module compact enough to mount on the UGOT chassis without hitting its wheels or the floor. From research: Aa
5.2 The robot and its test course fit on one standard classroom table (about 1.2 m × 0.8 m).The unit is tested at a normal desk in a single lesson, with no workshop machines.
Safety6.1 Rotating parts are enclosed or sit at least 6 mm clear of any pinch point.Reduces the chance of injury to the student building, testing and demonstrating it. From research: Ad, Af
6.2 No exposed sharp corners: all edges filleted or sanded; sensor lens recessed about 2 mm to avoid impact cracks.Standard safe-handling requirement for a student-built robotic part. From research: Af
Function7.1 Turns to face a clap or voice within 2 seconds from up to 3 m away.Directly measures whether the design solves the problem in the scenario above. From research: Aa, Ac
7.2 Microphones are spaced at least 60 mm apart so the robot can compare arrival times.Sensor range figures are design targets; the housing must never block or shorten them. From research: Ab, Ad
7.3 The sensor's field of view is unobstructed by any part of the housing.A sensor that is blocked gives wrong data, so the design fails its main job.
7.4 Sensors and servos are replaceable with hand tools in under 2 minutes.Classroom robots are reused across cohorts, so parts will wear and break. From research: Ac
Material8.1 Uses only parts from the UGOT kit, plus recycled card for test props.Kit parts are reliable and already paid for; recycled card is free and quick to change. From research: Ab
8.2 Flat test props (cards, signs, tags) are printed on paper or card, not 3D-printed or laser-cut.Paper props take minutes, can be changed after every test and need no workshop machines.

B2 · Design Ideas 3 different ideas

Three ideas that differ in mechanism and form, not just looks. Each sketch is an isometric render of the 3D CAD model with the sensor and mount labelled.

Concept 1 — “Echo-Ring” SELECTED

Six-microphone halo

Six microphone pods sit evenly around a ring on a mast, so the robot hears all directions at the same time and compares levels to find the source.

Envelope 166.0 × 144.0 × 120.0 mm · ≈ 59 g · ≈ AED 27

Concept 2 — “Parabola-Ears”

Two servo-steered dish ears

Two big dish ears on servos swivel like an owl's face to focus sound. Elegant, but it must scan and the dishes are bulky.

Envelope 110.9 × 210.3 × 131.0 mm · ≈ 156 g · ≈ AED 37

Concept 3 — “Sonar-Turret”

Rotating horn turret

Four horn microphones on a sweeping turret listen in turn. Looks like a radar and is easy to print, but the sweep takes time.

Envelope 182.2 × 182.2 × 96.5 mm · ≈ 265 g · ≈ AED 69

Build and test the robot

No 3D printing or laser cutting. Use the ready UGOT kit, write the program in Python and test it against your B1 specification.

  1. Check the kit: UGOT transforming car, main controller (microphones + speaker), laptop. Tell your teacher about any missing part now.
  2. Confirm your B3 design: the kit parts stand in for the “Echo-Ring” sketch (six-microphone halo). The part you design is the program.
  3. Assemble with the kit's own instructions, then photograph the finished robot.
  4. Connect the laptop to the UGOT and run a short program: read the loudness and print it.
  5. Function-test against the B1 function criteria: Turns to face a clap or voice within 2 seconds from up to 3 m away. Record results in your portfolio.
  6. Carry on to Criterion C to plan, code, log and justify your changes.
