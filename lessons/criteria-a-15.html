A1 · Design need

Explain the problem in a real setting, with evidence, and justify why it needs a solution.

After a major incident, responders may not be able to reach casualties quickly. Getting to and helping a person in this situation has problems, including:

  1. Responders cannot always reach the casualty: Rubble, smoke and damaged buildings block the way or make it unsafe to enter.
  2. It is hard to see inside the site: In smoke or darkness responders cannot tell how many people need help or where they are.
  3. Supplies take a long time to arrive: A first-aid kit or water has to be carried in by hand through dangerous areas.
  4. Bystanders do not know what is happening: People near a moving machine or a rescue operation can be hurt if there are no warnings.
  5. Sticking-out parts catch on people: Equipment with arms, wings or cables can snag clothes or skin.

After major incidents, responders need to reach people quickly and safely, and technology that goes first can show what to expect (add a real source and the date you accessed it).

Why a solution is needed: Reaching a casualty with a picture and basic supplies sooner would help responders decide what to do and could save lives.

A3 · Existing products

Study a range of similar products. For each: what it is, what works, what does not, and what you will take from it.

1. Tracked rescue robot (EOD and rescue robots)

A tracked robot with an arm and cameras, used in bomb disposal and disaster response.

Works well
Goes where people cannot and shows a live picture.
Limits
Expensive and needs a trained operator.
What I will take from it
A live camera view and an arm.

2. AED delivery drone

A drone that carries a defibrillator to a person in cardiac arrest.

Works well
Gets supplies to the scene quickly.
Limits
Short range and flying restrictions.
What I will take from it
Carrying supplies to a person fast.

3. Telepresence robot

A wheeled screen that lets a remote person see and talk to people.

Works well
Two-way picture and sound.
Limits
Needs a flat floor and a network.
What I will take from it
A clear picture with sound.

4. Search camera on a pole

A camera on an extendable pole used by rescuers to look into voids.

Works well
Cheap and simple; shows hidden spaces.
Limits
Only as far as the pole reaches.
What I will take from it
Looking into spaces safely.

What this analysis tells me

These products come from very different fields, and none is designed for this client's exact setting. Comparing them shows many different ways of dealing with a similar problem. From them I will take:

  • Tracked rescue robot: A live camera view and an arm.
  • AED delivery drone: Carrying supplies to a person fast.
  • Telepresence robot: A clear picture with sound.
  • Search camera on a pole: Looking into spaces safely.

These ideas become the starting point for the three design concepts I sketch in B2.

A4 · Design brief

Summarise the problem, the client and your research. Cite the research codes and the B1 numbers in brackets, like (Ab and 7.1).

First responders who need to find casualties and bring them supplies after a major incident, in the site of a major incident in the UAE, face a real problem. After a major incident, responders may not be able to reach casualties quickly or see what the situation is. Supplies take time to arrive and nearby people may not be warned of what is happening. (taken from my A1 problem analysis). Reaching a casualty with a picture and basic supplies sooner would help responders decide what to do and could save lives.

My design brief is to develop a solution that helps first responders deal with this problem. From my research I found that A typical school first-aid kit weighed about 180 g (EXEMPLAR DATA) (Aa and 5.1, 7.1). I also found that A test card was readable at 2 m in normal light but not in dim light (EXEMPLAR DATA) (Ad and 7.2, 6.1). After studying a wide range of existing products I learned that disaster robots and drones have been used to look into collapsed buildings and to deliver supplies (secondary research) (Ab and 8.1, 7.2). My research shows that the solution needs to do the following. It must carry a 200 g medical kit for 5 m and deliver it within 300 mm of a person. (Ac and 7.1) It must stream a clear picture from up to 2 m away. (Ad and 7.2) It must warn people nearby with lights and sound, and have rounded edges with nothing that sticks out and snags. (Af and 6.1) It also has to be affordable and practical. It must cost no more than AED 70 in added parts. (Ae and 2.1) It must be possible to build and test in one 70-minute lesson. (Ae and 3.2) In my survey most responses preferred this colour scheme: rescue white (Ag and 1.1), so I will use it.

The point of the design is to help first responders and keep people safer. It is connected with the global context “Fairness and development” because it responds to a real need in the community. It shows the Key Concept “Communities” because the parts of a solution have to work together as one system, and the Related Concepts “Function” and “Adaptation” because the solution will be judged by how well it works for the people who use it. (Check these against your own unit planner.)

From brief to specification. The brief points to the numbers in B1 (for example 7.1 and 7.2), where each need becomes a measurable success criterion. Criterion A does not choose a solution: that happens in B2 and B3.
