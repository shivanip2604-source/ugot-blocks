Before you start

Orange = the part you are making now. Grey = parts you already made.

  1. Go to tinkercad.com, sign in and click Create → 3D Design.
  2. Bottom-right of the workplane set Snap grid = 1.0 mm.
  3. Shapes live in the Basic Shapes panel (Box, Cylinder, Sphere).
  4. Click a shape to see its handles (picture). You will use the white squares, the black cone and the curved arrows all the time.
  5. Useful keys: Ctrl+Z undo · Ctrl+D duplicate · Ctrl+G group · Ctrl+Shift+G ungroup · L align · D drop to workplane
💡 Z means height above the workplane. X and Y are sideways. All sizes are in millimetres.

The steps

Tick each box when you finish a step. If you get stuck, press Ctrl+Z and try that step again.

Part 1 · Build the 3D model (about 45 to 60 minutes)

1The mounting bracket (your first shape)

  1. In the right-hand Basic Shapes panel drag a Box onto the workplane.
  2. Click the box. Click each size number and type:
SettingType this
X (width)16
Y (length)50
Height50
  1. Leave it sitting on the workplane (Z = 0). Colour: dark grey.
✅ Check: the box is 16 × 50 × 50 mm and its bottom is on the workplane.
💡 Why stand it up? It is easier to stack parts upwards. At the very end you will lay the whole arm down flat (Step 8).

2The shoulder ball

  1. Drag a Sphere onto the workplane.
  2. Type the size: X 60, Y 60, Height 60. Set Steps to 20 in the shape panel so it looks round.
  3. Line it up: select the new shape and the shapes you already built (hold Shift and click, or drag a box around them). Click Align (or press L). Click the middle dot on the X row and the middle dot on the Y row. Do not touch the Z row. Both shapes may slide a little together. That is fine: they only need to be lined up with each other.
  4. Height: click the black cone ▲ above the shape and type the Z number below (or drag it up until it shows that number).
SettingType this
Z (bottom of ball)25
✅ Check: the ball's bottom is at Z = 25 and its top at Z = 85. The bracket top is Z = 50, so the ball overlaps the upper half of the bracket (that is correct).

Part 1b · The three claw fingers (the tricky bit, go slowly)

7aFinger base (finger A)

  1. Cylinder: X 16, Y 16, Height 43 (Sides 32).
  2. Line it up with the hub: select the new shape and the shapes you already built (hold Shift and click, or drag a box around them). Click Align (or press L). Click the middle dot on the X row and the middle dot on the Y row. Do not touch the Z row. Both shapes may slide a little together. That is fine: they only need to be lined up with each other.
  3. Height: click the black cone ▲ above the shape and type the Z number below (or drag it up until it shows that number).
  4. Move it sideways: with the cylinder selected, tap the ← arrow key 26 times (Snap grid must say 1.0 mm, bottom-right). It now sits 26 mm to the left of the hub's middle.
SettingType this
Z (bottom)230
Sideways26 mm to the left
✅ Check: from the top view (right picture) the finger sits at the edge of the hub, 26 mm from the middle. Its top is at Z = 273.

7bFinger tip (tilted 21°)

  1. Cylinder: X 12, Y 12, Height 33.4 (Sides 32).
  2. Tilt it: click the cylinder, then the curved rotate arrow at its side. Type 21 degrees so the top leans toward the middle of the hub, like a bent fingertip.
  3. Line it up: select the new shape and the shapes you already built (hold Shift and click, or drag a box around them). Click Align (or press L). Click the middle dot on the X row and the middle dot on the Y row. Do not touch the Z row. Both shapes may slide a little together. That is fine: they only need to be lined up with each other.
  4. Tap ← 20 times to put the tip over the finger base.
  5. Height: use the black cone and type 270.8 (the lowest point of the tilted tip).
  6. Select the base and the tip and press Ctrl+G to group them into one finger.
✅ Check: the tip continues the finger upward and leans inwards. In the left picture the tip leans toward the other parts, not away.
💡 If the tip leans the wrong way, change the angle to −21 (minus 21).

7cA helper disc, so fingers can turn around the arm

To make copies that spin around the arm (not around themselves) we add a temporary invisible disc that centres the turning point.

  1. Drag a Cylinder. In the shape panel choose Hole. Type X 68, Y 68, Height 1.
  2. Line it up: select the new shape and the shapes you already built (hold Shift and click, or drag a box around them). Click Align (or press L). Click the middle dot on the X row and the middle dot on the Y row. Do not touch the Z row. Both shapes may slide a little together. That is fine: they only need to be lined up with each other.
  3. Height: Z = 320 (above the fingers, so it cuts nothing).
  4. Select the finger group and the disc and press Ctrl+G.
✅ Check: the teal disc floats above the finger and is centred on the hub.
💡 The disc must not touch the finger. If the finger loses a slice, the disc is too low: raise it.

7dMake fingers B and C

  1. Select the group (finger + disc). Press Ctrl+D to duplicate it.
  2. Click the flat curved arrow on the workplane around the shape and type 120 degrees.
  3. Click the original group again, Ctrl+D, and rotate that copy 240 degrees.
  4. Select each of the three groups in turn and press Ctrl+Shift+G (Ungroup). Click each teal helper disc and press Delete (three discs).
✅ Check: from the top (right picture) the fingers are 120° apart, all tips lean towards the middle, and there are no helper discs left.
💡 If a copy ends up in the wrong place, press Ctrl+Z (undo) and repeat. You can also change the colour of the three fingers now.
Part 1c · Lay it flat and check it

8Lay the arm down

  1. Press Ctrl+A to select everything, then Ctrl+G to group.
  2. Click the group. Use the curved rotate arrow on the side to tip it over by 90° so the arm lies flat and the bracket is a flat plate on the left.
  3. Press D (Drop) so the model sits on the workplane.
✅ Check: the model's size should read about 306 × 61 × 64 mm. One finger points up and two point down (or the reverse: both are fine because the three fingers are identical).


9Compare with the teacher's model

  1. Download UGOT_T04_CHOSEN_Claw-Reach_FINAL.stl.
  2. In Tinkercad click Import → choose the file → units mm → Import.
  3. Give the imported model a different colour. Select both models and use Align (middle dots on X, Y and Z).
  4. Look closely: if your model is right, the two almost completely overlap. Small differences (under 2 mm) are fine.
  5. Delete the teacher's model when you finish.
✅ Check: your bracket, ball, tubes, hub and three fingers all line up with the imported model.
Part 2 · Make the 2D drawings

10Capture the three views

  1. Use the view cube (top-left of the workplane) and click FRONT. If you can right-click the cube and switch on Orthographic, do it: this removes perspective so the sizes look true.
  2. Take a screenshot (Windows: Win+Shift+S; Mac: Cmd+Shift+4).
  3. Repeat for TOP and RIGHT.
  4. Paste the three screenshots into a Google Slide / Doc side by side and crop them close.

Model answer: front, top and right views with dimensions. Yours will look like this once finished.

11Add the dimensions

Draw arrows with the line tool (arrow at both ends) and type the sizes. A good drawing has the three overall sizes and the size of every part:

PartDimension
Overall length306.3 mm
Overall width61 mm
Overall height64 mm
Bracket50 × 50 × 16 mm
BallØ 60 mm
Outer tubeØ 36 × 90 mm
Inner tubeØ 26 × 65 mm
Wrist hubØ 50 × 30 mm
Sensor podØ 20 × 14 mm
Finger baseØ 16 × 43 mm (×3)
Finger tipØ 12 × 33 mm, tilted 21° (×3)
💡 Ø means diameter (the distance across a circle). Every number needs the unit mm. Add a title, your name and the date.

12Save and export

  1. Rename the design (top-left) to Joint Rescue Claw-Reach – your name.
  2. Click Export → .STL and save it. This is the file a 3D printer slicer uses.
  3. If your teacher asks for a laser-cut bracket: Tinkercad can also export .SVG (a flat 2D outline) from the same Export menu.
  4. Upload your STL and your 2D drawing to the place your teacher tells you.

If something goes wrong

  • A shape moved when I used Align: that is normal, both shapes line up with each other. Check Z is still right.
  • The ball looks like a diamond: raise Steps to 20.
  • A shape is below the workplane: select it and press D, then set the height again.
  • The numbers will not type: click directly on the number box, not the handle.
